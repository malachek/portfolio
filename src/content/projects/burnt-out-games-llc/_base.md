    lead: Incorporating changed how we design, for the better.
    blocks:
      - id: how-incorporating-changed-the-work-text
        type: text
        md: |-
          A commercial release has to hold a player past two hours, because that's Steam's refund window. That single number shapes scope, pacing, and what counts as finished.

          It also means positioning the game on a market it has to survive in, which is why marketing and branding stopped being optional for the games and for the studio.
      - id: how-incorporating-changed-the-work-text-3
        type: text
        md: |-
          It changed how we work with people. Non-studio members joining a project sign NDAs and contracts now.

          Conner and I drafted the revenue model. Developer share is points-based:

          - Role points scale with seniority and quarters involved.
          - Active points come from standups attended against standups expected, with grace for missed ones.
          - A developer's share is their points over the total.

          Revenue splits 45% to developers, 40% to the company to keep it running, 10% to business administration, and 5% across everyone involved in the project and related ones.

          Building that model meant deciding what contribution actually means when nobody is drawing a salary. Seniority, tenure, and showing up were the three things we could measure without arguing about them later.
